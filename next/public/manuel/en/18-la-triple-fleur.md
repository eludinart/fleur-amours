# The Triple Flower

The Triple Flower Relational Mode (Couple, polyamory-friendly, multi-player game) Why play The Triple Flower? This game mode views the relationship as a shared garden. It allows each person to express what they are experiencing "for themselves" within the connection, and then to see what is woven "between us" through the common Flower. Using the tarot from this perspective means moving away from debates of ideas and engaging with images, symbols, and feelings. The cards offer a third language: they allow needs, wounds, desires, and impulses to be laid out without accusation or justification. Whether you are a couple, a triad, a quartet, or in another form of connection, The Triple Flower invites you to view your relational system as a living organism: to see what nourishes it, what exhausts it, and what you wish to cultivate together moving forward. This game mode is primarily designed for two people (a couple, two close friends, life partners...). However, it can also be played in a group, with up to 6 people. In all cases, the principle remains the same: - each person can compose their individual Flower, - the group can compose a common Flower, which speaks of the relationship itself, - or both, first the individual flowers and then the common flower. At the end of the game, you obtain: a Flower for each person, a common Flower for the connection (or relational system) you form together. Intention of this mode The Triple Flower allows you to: see how each person positions themselves in the relationship, see what truly connects you today, name what you wish to nurture, transform, or protect. This can be an emotionally powerful game. Take your time, breathe, and keep in mind that the cards speak of dynamics, not definitive judgments. 34 Materials needed The complete Fleur d'ÅmÔurs Tarot (65 cards). A space on the table for: Person A's Flower, Person B's Flower (and possibly C, D...), the common Flower in the center, (Optional) A notebook or sheets of paper to jot down emerging insights, Draw pile, discard pile, and Seedbed The draw pile: face-down cards from which you draw. The discard pile: These are not cards discarded "forever"; each discarded card is placed face-down beneath the draw pile. In practice: When the text says "discard a card," it means: place that card face-down beneath the draw pile. The draw pile and discard pile thus form a single, living pile that recycles continuously, a kind of compost bin. The card placed in the center is called the Seedbed: Turn over the first card of the draw pile and place it face-up in the center; it sets the general tone for the game. Setup Each person draws 8 cards and keeps them in hand or lays them out in front of them. Form the draw pile with the remaining cards, face-down. Turn over the first card of the draw pile in the center: This is the Seedbed, the "theme of the garden" for this game. You can take a moment to state aloud what this Seedbed evokes for your relationship. Ensure you have space for: an "individual Flower" area for each person, a "common Flower" area in the center. Open or closed play The Triple Flower can be played in open or closed mode. In open mode, each player can lay out all 8 of their cards, visible to everyone. This encourages more discussion about meanings, interpretations, and each person's needs, and facilitates proposals for bartering and resource exchange between players. 35 Always 8 cards in hand: Each player starts with 8 cards in hand. Whenever a barter, gift, or exchange takes place, the player who ends up with fewer than 8 cards immediately replenishes their hand by drawing from the Draw pile. Freedom of play In The Triple Flower, freedom of play takes precedence over rules. As long as exchanges remain lively and contribute to the formation of a flourishing Flower, the group is free to adjust, simplify, or complicate the rules. Any variation is considered valid if it serves dialogue, clarity about what each person is experiencing, and the quality of connections rather than a rigid framework. Phase 1 – Exploring your hand: Me, Us, Not for this game Each person looks at their 8 cards and mentally or physically categorizes them into three groups: "Me": cards that primarily speak to my way of being in this relationship, "Us": cards that seem to speak primarily of the connection, the dynamic between us, "Not for this game": cards that don't really resonate for this time. You can lay the cards out in front of you in three small piles, without yet forming them into a Flower. Adjusting your hand with the draw pile To refine, each person can make up to 3 exchanges with the draw pile: For each possible exchange: Draw a card from the top of the draw pile and look at it. Decide: either to keep it and discard another card from your hand (sliding it face-down under the draw pile), or to discard it directly (face-down, under the draw pile). The goal is not to "find the right card," but to adjust the hand so that it aligns with the Seedbed and the situation. If there are 3 or 4 of you, each person does the same, asking themselves: "What do these cards tell me about my place in this relational system, and about what we are creating together?" 36 Phase 2 – Individual Flowers Each person will now compose their individual Flower, using their "Me" pile and any cards that have been offered or traded. Choose 3 to 5 cards from your "Me" pile that feel most accurate to describe yourself in the relationship. You can keep more if you feel it's right, but 5 cards remain readable. Arrange them in front of you in the shape of a Flower: in a circle, or intuitively connecting them to certain ÅmÔurs shapes (see the Flower on the cover); the important thing is that you feel "this is your Flower for this moment." In turn, each person presents their Flower: explaining why a particular card is there, what it says about their way of loving, their needs, their fears, their impulses. The others listen without correcting or interpreting for them. This is not a debate; it is a moment of formation and mutual recognition. Phase 3 – The Common Flower Once each person has their individual Flower, you will create a common Flower for the relationship. Each player takes back their "Us" pile. They choose 2 to 3 cards from it that they feel ready to offer to the common Flower: cards that speak of what you are experiencing together, what you are creating, your shared challenges, or your wishes for the future. Pool these chosen cards and discuss: Which cards resonate most with who we are today? Which ones represent more of a wish, a direction, a potential to nurture? Together, compose the common Flower in the center of the table, using 4 to 8 cards: "strength" cards: what supports the relationship, "tension" cards: where friction occurs, "intention" cards: what you wish to honor or adjust, cards with double resonance: me, you, us. 37 Some cards will speak clearly on multiple levels: to you, to the other, and to the relationship. To keep the game simple: A card is physically placed in only one Flower (individual or common). However, you can state that it resonates on multiple levels: "We'll put it in the common Flower, but I also feel it very strongly for myself." "This card is in my Flower, but I also see it as a central issue for the couple." You can place a small stone, a seed, or make a mark in your notebook to indicate these "double-meaning" cards. Closing the game Look at the three (or more) Flowers together: each person's Flower, the common Flower. Some possible questions to conclude: What touches you most in your individual Flower? What do you discover seeing the other's Flower? Which cards from the common Flower inspire you to nurture, which invite you to set boundaries, which open up a desire or a project? You can also return to the initial Seedbed: "With this Seedbed, and what we see in the Flowers, what do we want to water in our relationship, and what do we want to let compost?" 38 In Conclusion When The Triple Flower closes, nothing is truly finished. The cards have played their role as messengers and then fall silent. What remains are words that have found their rightful place, emotions acknowledged without being dissected, resonances shared without being fixed. Each person leaves with a fragment of the shared experience: an image, a phrase, an inner shift. The Triple Flower does not aim to produce an answer, but to open a space where life can circulate freely, be expressed, and sometimes transform, simply because it has been welcomed. 39
