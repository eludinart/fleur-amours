The Dance of the Cards (multiplayer game) Introduction When the cards take form The Dance of the Cards is a collective game of the Fleur d'ÅmÔurs Tarot that chooses a direct path: to go through the body. Here, the cards are not first commented on or mentally "understood": they are embodied. Posture, breath, distance, rhythm, gaze... everything becomes language. And often, this language speaks more truthfully than words, because it touches the living, instinctive, pre-verbal layer. It is a practice without winners or losers, which can be experienced by 2 to 6 people (up to 8 if the space is good and the framework is maintained). The Dance of the Cards transforms a reading into an experience: each person composes a mini Fleur of the moment, then sets it in motion—for themselves, and with others—until an adjustment is felt, naturally, within the group. Objective To compose a Fleur d'ÅmÔurs of three to eight cards in symbolic coherence before oneself (possibility of using the Triple Fleur game mode or simply an open or closed reading). This flower represents an inner state, a relational vision, or a life movement revealed throughout the game. Setup Ideally, practice standing, in a circle, in a clear space. A few details make a real difference: Space: no obstacles on the floor, enough room to open your arms and move without bumping into each other. Atmosphere: music is possible, but never mandatory. Silence works very well. If music: low volume, no lyrics if it's distracting. Framework: one person holds the framework (even if it's among friends). Safety rules: zero performance (you don't dance "well," you dance truly), explicit consent (no contact without a clear "yes"), right to minimal amplitude (a micro-gesture is enough), agreed-upon stop signal (raised hand / keyword) = immediate pause, confidentiality (what is experienced here stays within the circle). Start with 1 to 3 very simple minutes: three breaths together, a mini warm-up (neck/shoulders/pelvis/hands), then an intention in one sentence. 40 Flow (typical scenario) The game unfolds in waves: individual, then collective, then integration. 1) Intention The group sets a short sentence that gives direction. Examples: "Explore how love circulates between us tonight." "Clarify a tension without explaining ourselves for an hour." "Reconnect as a couple." 2) Drawing the cards (e.g., personal mini Fleur) Each person draws or chooses three cards (this is the most fluid format). They compose a mini Fleur of the moment. A simple grid can be used (optional), for example: State (how I feel) Need (what is missing / what calls) Impulse (what I bring / what I offer) Initially, you can set the words aside: you don't justify your cards. Let them first pass through the body. 3) Individual dance: "dancing one's flower" In turn, each person enters the center (or takes a step forward in the circle) and embodies their cards. This can be: a held posture, a repeated movement, a walk, a sway, a gesture that arises and returns. No choreography is needed: sincerity is enough. During this time, the others watch in silence, as one listens to a story. No comments, no interruptions, just reception. 4) Collective mirror: feeling together After someone's dance, the group can take up a gesture or a quality of movement (with the person's agreement). The idea is not to imitate, but to feel: to let the symbol pass into other bodies, and to observe what it activates. 5) Common dance: the group's resonance Then, the group enters into a free dance together. This is often where something is revealed: synchronizations, distances readjusting, impulses meeting, resistances appearing then releasing. Sometimes, a kind of spontaneous "choreography" emerges—not manufactured, but found. 41 6) Landing + simple words When the energy naturally subsides, return to the circle. A short silence, then a few words from each person, in "I" statements: "I felt..." — "I noticed..." — "It touched me here..." You can conclude with a final card or flower, not as a verdict, but as an anchor. Why it works The body bypasses mental strategies: relational truths emerge without "discourse." Movement anchors: a card becomes a reference gesture that can be revisited later. In a group, empathy becomes concrete: you feel the co-resonance (emotions, rhythms, distances) and what the collective regulates together. Role of the facilitator Protect the framework (consent, rhythm, safety). Remind of the golden rule: experience before interpretation. Bring back to the body as soon as the mind races. Keep durations simple (avoid it becoming endless or confusing). Classic pitfalls (to avoid) The stage: performing instead of inhabiting. Antidote: "minimal movement accepted." Intrusion: non-consensual contact. Antidote: explicit consent. Wild interpretation: diagnosing the other. Antidote: "I speak about myself." 42 Combining games, moving from one language to another The Fleur d'ÅmÔurs Tarot was designed as a living system: its games and readings are not separate corridors, but doors that can connect. You can therefore combine forms of play to make the same Fleur travel through several languages: symbolic, corporeal, creative. The idea is simple: a first game reveals a structure (what is there), a second game sets it in motion (what wants to circulate), a third game anchors (what wants to remain). Examples of natural combinations Triple Fleur / Dance of the Cards You build a Triple Fleur (personal + other's perspective + common flower), then you dance this flower: what was "seen" becomes "lived." The dance quickly shows where it opens, where it blocks, where it seeks the right distance. Triple Fleur / Drawing / Painting After the Triple Fleur, instead of talking for a long time, you draw it (mandala, petals, symbols) or paint it. This transforms the reading into a tangible trace: an image that can accompany you for several weeks. Individual Fleur / Dance / Writing You draw your mini Fleur, you dance it, then you write for 10 minutes without filter: what I felt, what I didn't dare to say, what I ask of love now. Writing serves as a landing. Short reading / Collective creation In a group, you do a common flower, then you create an "echo work": collage, mural, group poetry, symbolic altar, choreography, land art. The reading becomes an act of cohesion. The golden rules: keep it simple, invent your own rules. Combining does not mean stacking. A good combination consists of two steps (sometimes three): Mapping (reading / flower), Embodying (dance / theater / gesture), Anchoring (drawing / painting / writing). This is often where the magic becomes concrete: the Fleur is not just a reading, it is a process. 43 44 45
